Appendix A - HORA-TAI Readiness Assessment Worksheet
====================================================

A.1 Purpose
-----------

This appendix provides an illustrative worksheet for documenting the assessment of human and organizational readiness for a defined delegation of authority to an agentic AI system.

The worksheet is intended to support structured assessment, documentation, review, and reassessment.

It may be adapted to the needs of different sectors, organizations, applications, and regulatory environments.

The worksheet does not constitute a certification instrument or legal determination.

A.2 Assessment Identification
-----------------------------

**Assessment identifier**

Record a unique identifier for the assessment.

**Assessment date**

Record the date on which the assessment is performed.

**Assessment version**

Record the version of the assessment methodology used.

**Assessment owner**

Identify the person or organizational function responsible for conducting and maintaining the assessment.

**Review authority**

Identify the person or organizational function responsible for reviewing and approving the assessment.

A.3 Delegation Context
----------------------

**Delegating party**

Identify the human, team, organizational unit, or organization authorizing the delegation.

**Agent identifier**

Identify the AI agent or agentic AI system being assessed.

**Agent version**

Record the relevant agent, model, configuration, or deployment version where available.

**Delegation objective**

Describe the objective for which authority is being delegated.

**Operating environment**

Describe the systems, tools, data, services, processes, and external environments with which the agent may interact.

**Affected parties**

Identify individuals, organizations, systems, or other entities that may be affected by the agent's activity.

A.4 Delegated Authority
-----------------------

Document the authority proposed for delegation.

**Permitted actions**

List the actions the agent is authorized to perform.

**Prohibited actions**

List actions the agent is explicitly prohibited from performing.

**Decision authority**

Describe the decisions the agent may make independently.

**System access**

Identify systems, applications, information, tools, and services accessible to the agent.

**Transaction limits**

Identify applicable financial, operational, information, or other limits.

**Duration**

Specify the period during which the authority is valid.

**Escalation conditions**

Define the conditions under which the agent must request human or organizational intervention.

**Revocation conditions**

Define the conditions under which delegated authority may be reduced, suspended, or terminated.

A.5 Proposed Delegation Level
-----------------------------

Record the proposed HORA-TAI delegation level.

**Level 0 - Advisory**

The agent provides information, analysis, recommendations, or proposed actions without independently executing consequential actions.

**Level 1 - Constrained Execution**

The agent executes narrowly defined actions within explicit and predetermined constraints.

**Level 2 - Conditional Autonomy**

The agent performs multiple actions and makes intermediate decisions within predefined conditions and escalation rules.

**Level 3 - Extended Autonomy**

The agent pursues a defined objective through multiple steps with broader decision authority and limited direct human intervention.

**Level 4 - High-Consequence Autonomy**

The agent exercises substantial autonomy in an activity where unintended or incorrect actions could produce significant consequences.

The selected level should be justified in relation to the actual delegated authority and operating environment.

A.6 Agent Capability Assessment
------------------------------

Assess the capabilities relevant to the proposed delegation.

Consider:

* reasoning capability;
* planning capability;
* tool use;
* external-system interaction;
* memory or state management;
* instruction following;
* ability to recognize uncertainty;
* ability to escalate;
* ability to operate within defined boundaries;
* known limitations;
* known failure modes; and
* relevant evaluation evidence.

**Assessment result**

Record whether the agent capability is:

* sufficient;
* sufficient with controls;
* insufficient; or
* requires further evidence.

**Evidence**

Record the evidence supporting the determination.

A.7 Human Capability and Understanding
--------------------------------------

Assess whether the relevant humans possess sufficient capability to supervise the delegation.

Consider:

* AI literacy;
* understanding of the agent's purpose;
* understanding of agent capabilities;
* understanding of agent limitations;
* ability to interpret agent activity;
* ability to recognize unexpected behavior;
* judgment capability;
* intervention capability;
* escalation capability; and
* awareness of applicable responsibilities.

**Assessment result**

Record:

* capability established;
* capability established with conditions;
* capability gap identified; or
* assessment incomplete.

**Evidence**

Record supporting evidence.

**Remediation**

Document any training, competency development, or other remediation required.

A.8 Meaningful Human Oversight
------------------------------

Assess whether human oversight is effective in practice.

Consider:

* visibility of agent activity;
* availability of relevant information;
* human workload;
* decision authority;
* intervention mechanisms;
* intervention speed;
* escalation mechanisms;
* monitoring arrangements;
* ability to suspend delegation; and
* ability to understand material agent actions.

**Oversight determination**

Record whether oversight is:

* meaningful and effective;
* meaningful with conditions;
* insufficient; or
* not yet established.

**Evidence**

Document tests, procedures, monitoring records, simulations, or other evidence.

A.9 Delegation Governance
-------------------------

Assess the organizational governance surrounding the delegation.

Consider:

* delegation policy;
* approval process;
* role definitions;
* risk assessment;
* change management;
* escalation procedures;
* incident management;
* suspension procedures;
* termination procedures; and
* management oversight.

**Governance determination**

Record:

* established;
* established with conditions;
* partially established; or
* not established.

A.10 Accountability
-------------------

Identify the relevant accountability roles.

**Delegating party**

Identify who authorizes the delegation.

**Supervising human or function**

Identify who is responsible for oversight.

**Operational owner**

Identify who is responsible for the operational deployment.

**Agent operator**

Identify who manages the agent operationally.

**Incident owner**

Identify who is responsible for managing significant incidents.

**Escalation authority**

Identify who has authority to intervene, modify, suspend, or terminate delegation.

**Review authority**

Identify who reviews significant outcomes and reassesses readiness.

A.11 Authorization and Boundary Controls
----------------------------------------

Assess whether delegated authority is appropriately constrained.

Consider:

* authentication;
* authorization;
* least-privilege access;
* action restrictions;
* transaction limits;
* information-access limits;
* time restrictions;
* approval requirements;
* escalation thresholds;
* emergency controls; and
* authority revocation.

**Control determination**

Record whether controls are:

* sufficient;
* sufficient with conditions;
* insufficient; or
* not established.

A.12 Workforce and Organizational Capability
--------------------------------------------

Assess whether the organization has sufficient workforce and organizational capability.

Consider:

* AI literacy;
* role-specific training;
* technical expertise;
* governance expertise;
* risk management;
* operational support;
* incident response;
* staffing;
* workload;
* organizational change management; and
* leadership support.

**Capability determination**

Record:

* sufficient;
* sufficient with conditions;
* capability gap identified; or
* assessment incomplete.

Identify the records required to support accountability and assurance.

Consider whether the organization can reconstruct:

* delegation authorization;

* relevant instructions;
* applicable policies;
* significant decisions;
* material actions;
* external interactions;
* human interventions;
* escalations;
* exceptions;
* outcomes; and
* changes to authority.

**Traceability determination**

Record:

* sufficient;
* sufficient with conditions;
* insufficient; or
* not established.

A.14 Continuous Assurance
-------------------------

Identify how readiness will be monitored after delegation begins.

Consider:

* performance monitoring;
* incident monitoring;
* near-miss monitoring;
* capability changes;
* organizational changes;
* workforce changes;
* risk changes;
* policy changes;
* system changes;
* periodic review; and
* event-driven reassessment.

**Reassessment frequency**

Specify the planned reassessment interval.

**Reassessment triggers**

Identify events that require reassessment before the scheduled review.

A.15 Readiness Gap Register
---------------------------

Record identified gaps using the following structure.

**Gap identifier**

Assign a unique identifier.

**Readiness dimension**

Identify the affected readiness dimension.

**Gap description**

Describe the missing or insufficient capability or control.

**Potential consequence**

Describe the potential effect of the gap.

**Required remediation**

Describe the action required.

**Responsible party**

Identify the person or function responsible for remediation.

**Target date**

Specify the target completion date.

**Status**

Record:

* open;
* in progress;
* remediated; or
* accepted with documented justification.

A.16 Overall Readiness Determination
------------------------------------

The overall determination should consider the relationship between the proposed delegation and the evidence collected across the readiness dimensions.

The determination should not be based solely on an aggregate numerical score.

The following descriptive outcomes may be used:

**Proceed**

The assessed conditions are sufficient for the proposed delegation under the defined controls.

**Proceed with Conditions**

The delegation may proceed subject to specified controls, remediation, monitoring, or additional oversight.

**Reduce Delegation**

The delegated authority should be narrowed until readiness conditions are strengthened.

**Defer Delegation**

Delegation should not begin until identified readiness gaps have been addressed.

**Suspend Delegation**

Existing delegated authority should be temporarily suspended because readiness or operating conditions are no longer sufficient.

**Terminate Delegation**

Delegated authority should be withdrawn because continued delegation is no longer appropriate.

A.17 Assurance Statement
-----------------------

The assessment record should include a concise statement describing the basis for the readiness determination.

The statement should identify:

* the delegation assessed;
* the evidence reviewed;
* material readiness gaps;
* controls applied;
* oversight arrangements;
* accountability arrangements;
* reassessment conditions; and
* the resulting delegation decision.

A.19 Reassessment
-----------------

The assessment should be reviewed when:

* the agent's capabilities materially change;
* the agent receives new tools or system access;
* delegated authority expands;
* organizational responsibilities change;
* workforce capabilities materially change;
* a significant incident occurs;
* a significant near miss occurs;
* oversight effectiveness deteriorates;
* applicable requirements change;
* operating conditions materially change; or
* the defined review period expires.

A.21 Relationship to Assurance Records
--------------------------------------

The worksheet may provide the human-readable basis for a structured assurance record.

A future machine-readable representation may enable organizations to exchange selected readiness information across systems while preserving appropriate security, privacy, authorization, and governance controls.

Such a representation could include:

* assessment identifier;
* delegation identifier;
* agent identifier;
* delegation level;
* readiness dimensions;
* evidence references;
* controls;
* accountability roles;
* decision;
* validity period; and
* reassessment conditions.

The design of such a representation is outside the current scope of this appendix and may be addressed in future work.

A.22 Limitations
----------------

This worksheet is an initial proposal intended to support discussion and practical experimentation.

It does not establish:

* a universal numerical readiness score;
* a universal certification threshold;
* legal responsibility;
* sector-specific regulatory compliance;
* a mandatory audit methodology; or
* a determination that any particular delegation is inherently safe or unsafe.

Future versions should be informed by empirical validation, stakeholder consultation, practical deployments, and compatibility with relevant AI governance and assurance approaches.
