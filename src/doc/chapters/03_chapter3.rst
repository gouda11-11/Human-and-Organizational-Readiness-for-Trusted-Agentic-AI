3 Terms and Definitions
=======================

For the purposes of this specification, the following terms and definitions apply.

3.1 Agentic AI
--------------

**agentic AI**

An artificial intelligence system capable of pursuing a defined objective through one or more autonomous or semi-autonomous processes that may include interpreting objectives, planning, reasoning, selecting actions, using tools, interacting with external systems, and executing actions within an authorized scope.

An agentic AI system may operate with different degrees of autonomy and may require different levels of human oversight depending on the nature and consequences of its activities.

3.2 AI Agent
------------

**AI agent**

An AI-enabled software or computational entity that can perceive relevant information, interpret objectives, determine or select actions, and execute one or more actions within a defined operational environment.

An AI agent may use one or more AI models, tools, data sources, software services, or external systems.

3.3 Delegation
-------------

**delegation**

The act by which a human or organization authorizes an AI agent to perform defined activities, make defined intermediate decisions, or take defined actions on behalf of the delegating party.

Delegation does not necessarily transfer ultimate accountability for the delegated activity.

3.4 Delegated Authority
-----------------------

**delegated authority**

The defined set of permissions, decision rights, actions, or operational capabilities that a human or organization authorizes an AI agent to exercise within specified conditions.

Delegated authority should have a defined scope, purpose, duration or validity condition, constraints, and applicable oversight and escalation requirements.

3.5 Delegation Boundary
-----------------------

**delegation boundary**

The defined limit within which an AI agent is authorized to act.

A delegation boundary may specify permitted actions, prohibited actions, financial or operational limits, information-access constraints, time limits, escalation conditions, approval requirements, or other controls.

3.6 Delegation Lifecycle
-----------------------

**delegation lifecycle**

The sequence of activities associated with establishing, activating, monitoring, modifying, reviewing, suspending, and terminating delegated authority.

3.7 Human Oversight
-------------------

**human oversight**

The involvement of a human actor or designated human governance function in supervising, reviewing, controlling, or intervening in the operation of an AI system.

3.8 Meaningful Human Oversight
------------------------------

**meaningful human oversight**

Human oversight that provides a genuine and effective ability to understand relevant agent activity, assess material risks or outcomes, intervene where appropriate, and exercise control over delegated authority.

Meaningful human oversight requires more than the nominal presence of a human in an AI-enabled process.

The effectiveness of oversight depends on factors including information availability, human capability, decision authority, intervention mechanisms, timing, workload, and the consequences of the agent's actions.

3.9 Human Readiness
-------------------

**human readiness**

The capability of an individual human actor to understand, supervise, interact with, and appropriately exercise control over an agentic AI system within a defined delegation context.

Human readiness may include knowledge, skills, situational awareness, judgment, understanding of AI limitations, intervention capability, and awareness of applicable responsibilities.

3.10 Organizational Readiness
-----------------------------

**organizational readiness**

The capability of an organization to govern, deploy, supervise, monitor, evaluate, and continuously assure agentic AI systems within defined operational and risk conditions.

Organizational readiness may include governance structures, policies, workforce capabilities, accountability arrangements, technical controls, monitoring capabilities, incident processes, and assurance mechanisms.

3.11 Readiness
--------------

**readiness**

The degree to which the relevant human and organizational capabilities, controls, processes, governance arrangements, and resources are sufficient to support a defined delegation of authority to an agentic AI system.

Readiness is context-dependent and should be assessed in relation to the specific agent, delegated authority, operating environment, and potential consequences of agent actions.

3.12 Readiness Dimension
------------------------

**readiness dimension**

A defined category of human or organizational capability used to assess readiness for a particular delegation of authority.

Examples include human capability, oversight effectiveness, governance, accountability, workforce readiness, traceability, and continuous assurance.

3.13 Delegation Level
--------------------

**delegation level**

A descriptive category representing the relative degree of authority or autonomy delegated to an AI agent within a defined context.

Delegation levels are used by this specification as a common vocabulary for discussing differences in delegated authority. They are not intended to constitute a universal risk classification.

3.14 Autonomy
------------

**autonomy**

The degree to which an AI agent can determine and execute intermediate decisions or actions without requiring direct human approval for each individual step.

Autonomy may vary within a single deployment and should be understood in relation to the authority granted to the agent.

3.15 Human Agency
-----------------

**human agency**

The ability of a human actor or group of human actors to understand relevant choices, exercise meaningful control, make decisions, intervene in AI-mediated processes, and retain appropriate decision rights.

3.16 Accountability
------------------

**accountability**

The condition in which responsibility for decisions, actions, processes, or outcomes can be assigned to an identifiable human or organizational actor within the applicable governance and legal framework.

Delegating an activity to an AI agent does not, by itself, eliminate the need for identifiable accountability.

3.17 Traceability
-----------------

**traceability**

The ability to establish and reconstruct relevant relationships between an agent's inputs, instructions, authorization, intermediate actions, outputs, external actions, human interventions, and resulting outcomes to a degree appropriate to the context.

3.18 Authorization
------------------

**authorization**

The process of determining and granting the permissions or authority that an entity may exercise within a defined system, process, or operational environment.

In the context of this specification, authorization may determine the actions an AI agent is permitted to perform within a delegation boundary.

3.19 Intervention
-----------------

**intervention**

An action by an authorized human or organizational control function to modify, constrain, pause, suspend, redirect, or terminate an AI agent's operation or delegated authority.

3.20 Escalation
--------------

**escalation**

The process by which an agentic AI system transfers a decision, action, exception, uncertainty, or risk condition to an authorized human or organizational function because predefined conditions for autonomous execution have not been satisfied.

3.21 Continuous Assurance
-------------------------

**continuous assurance**

An ongoing process of evaluating whether an AI agent, its delegated authority, the relevant human and organizational capabilities, and associated controls remain appropriate as operating conditions, capabilities, risks, policies, and environments change.

3.22 Assurance
-------------

**assurance**

A structured basis for establishing confidence that specified requirements, controls, capabilities, and governance conditions are present and operating as intended.

3.23 Trustworthy Delegation
--------------------------

**trustworthy delegation**

A delegation relationship in which the scope and conditions of delegated authority are appropriately defined, the agent and its operating environment have appropriate safeguards, the relevant humans and organization are sufficiently ready to supervise and govern the delegation, and accountability and intervention mechanisms are established.

3.24 Delegating Party
--------------------

**delegating party**

The human or organization that grants defined authority to an AI agent.

The delegating party may be an individual, team, organization, or authorized organizational function, depending on the deployment context.

3.25 Supervising Human
----------------------

**supervising human**

A human actor assigned responsibility for monitoring, reviewing, interacting with, or intervening in the operation of an AI agent within a defined delegation context.

3.26 Agent Operator
------------------

**agent operator**

A human or organizational function responsible for the operational management of an AI agent, including activities such as configuration, monitoring, maintenance, access management, and operational response.

3.27 Consequential Action
------------------------

**consequential action**

An action or decision performed or initiated by an AI agent that may produce material effects for an individual, organization, system, transaction, resource, or other affected party.

The degree of consequence is context-dependent.

3.28 Reversibility
------------------

**reversibility**

The degree to which an action performed by an AI agent can be undone, corrected, or otherwise restored without material residual impact.

3.29 Delegation Context
----------------------

**delegation context**

The combination of the agent, delegated authority, human and organizational actors, operating environment, applicable controls, objectives, risks, and conditions under which delegation occurs.

3.30 Readiness Gap
------------------

**readiness gap**

The difference between the human and organizational capabilities required for a defined delegation and the capabilities, controls, processes, or resources actually available.

A readiness gap may indicate that delegated authority should be reduced, additional safeguards introduced, human capability strengthened, or delegation deferred.

3.31 Agentic AI System
---------------------

**agentic AI system**

A system composed of one or more AI agents and associated models, software, tools, data sources, interfaces, services, controls, and operational components that collectively enable the system to pursue defined objectives and execute actions within an operational environment.

3.32 Affected Party
------------------

**affected party**

An individual, organization, community, system, or other entity that may experience consequences from an AI agent's decisions, recommendations, actions, or omissions.

3.33 Delegation Decision
-----------------------

**delegation decision**

A decision by an authorized human or organization to establish, modify, continue, reduce, suspend, or terminate delegated authority.

3.34 Readiness Assessment
------------------------

**readiness assessment**

A structured evaluation of whether the relevant human and organizational capabilities, controls, governance arrangements, and resources are sufficient for a defined delegation of authority.

3.35 Assurance Record
--------------------

**assurance record**

A structured record containing evidence relevant to establishing and maintaining confidence that a defined delegation satisfies applicable readiness, oversight, governance, and assurance requirements.
