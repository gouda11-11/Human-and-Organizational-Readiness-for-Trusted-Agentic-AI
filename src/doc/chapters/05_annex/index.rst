Annex A - HORA-TAI Delegation and Readiness Assessment
=======================================================

A.1 Purpose
-----------

This annex provides an illustrative assessment approach for applying the Human and Organizational Readiness Framework for Trusted Agentic AI (HORA-TAI).

The assessment approach is intended to support structured discussion and evaluation of whether a defined delegation of authority is appropriate given the capabilities of the agent and the readiness of the relevant human and organization.

The approach is not intended to constitute a universal certification scheme or a legally binding determination.

A.2 Assessment Principle
------------------------

The assessment should evaluate the relationship between:

* the authority proposed for delegation;
* the capabilities and limitations of the agent;
* the potential consequences of agent actions;
* the reversibility of those actions;
* the human oversight available;
* the organizational governance environment;
* the workforce capabilities available;
* the accountability arrangements;
* the technical and organizational controls; and
* the assurance evidence available.

The assessment should be specific to the delegation context.

A.3 Assessment Sequence
-----------------------

A HORA-TAI assessment may follow the sequence below.

**Step 1 - Define the delegation**

Identify the objective, activities, decisions, actions, systems, information, and external interactions included within the proposed delegation.

**Step 2 - Define the authority boundary**

Specify what the agent may do, what it may not do, when it may act, what limits apply, and what conditions require human approval or escalation.

**Step 3 - Determine the proposed delegation level**

Assign an initial delegation level using the descriptive levels defined in Chapter 4.

**Step 4 - Assess agent capability**

Evaluate whether the agent possesses the capabilities required for the proposed delegation and identify relevant limitations and failure modes.

**Step 5 - Assess human readiness**

Evaluate whether the relevant humans possess the knowledge, skills, authority, information, and capacity required for effective oversight.

**Step 6 - Assess organizational readiness**

Evaluate governance, workforce capability, accountability, monitoring, incident response, assurance, and other organizational conditions.

**Step 7 - Assess oversight effectiveness**

Determine whether humans can meaningfully observe, understand, intervene in, and control agent activity where required.

**Step 8 - Identify readiness gaps**

Identify capabilities or controls that are missing, insufficient, outdated, or otherwise inappropriate for the proposed delegation.

**Step 9 - Determine controls and remediation**

Establish additional controls, narrower authority, additional training, stronger oversight, or other remediation where required.

**Step 10 - Determine delegation outcome**

Determine whether delegation may proceed, proceed subject to conditions, be reduced, be deferred, or not proceed.

**Step 11 - Establish reassessment conditions**

Define the events, time periods, capability changes, incidents, or other conditions that require reassessment.

A.5 Readiness Dimension Assessment
----------------------------------

Each readiness dimension may be assessed using a structured evidence-based approach.

A five-point descriptive scale may be used for internal assessment:

* **0 - Not established:** The required capability or control is absent or cannot be demonstrated.
* **1 - Initial:** The capability exists in limited or informal form.
* **2 - Defined:** The capability is formally defined but implementation may be incomplete or inconsistent.
* **3 - Operational:** The capability is implemented and operating under normal conditions.
* **4 - Assured:** The capability is operational, evidenced, monitored, and periodically evaluated.

The scale is intended as an assessment aid and does not constitute a universal maturity model.

A.6 Human Capability Assessment
------------------------------

Assessment should consider whether the relevant humans can:

* understand the purpose and operating context of the agent;
* understand material capabilities and limitations;
* interpret relevant outputs and actions;
* recognize potential errors or anomalies;
* understand delegated authority;
* exercise judgment about agent activity;
* intervene when required;
* escalate issues appropriately; and
* suspend or modify delegation when authorized.

Evidence may include training records, competency assessments, exercises, operational experience, and documented procedures.

A.7 Meaningful Oversight Assessment
-----------------------------------

Assessment should consider whether:

* relevant agent actions are observable;
* relevant information is available to the responsible human;
* the human has sufficient time and capacity to supervise;
* the human can distinguish normal from abnormal behavior;
* intervention is technically possible;
* intervention authority is clearly assigned;
* escalation mechanisms are operational; and
* intervention can occur within a timeframe appropriate to the consequences of the action.

An oversight process should not be considered meaningful solely because a human is nominally assigned to monitor the system.

A.8 Delegation Boundary Assessment
----------------------------------

The assessment should establish whether:

* the delegated objective is clearly defined;
* permitted actions are explicitly specified;
* prohibited actions are identified;
* authority limits are defined;
* access to systems and information is appropriately restricted;
* approval conditions are defined;
* escalation thresholds are established;
* emergency controls exist; and
* delegated authority can be modified or revoked.

A.9 Accountability Assessment
-----------------------------

The assessment should identify:

* the delegating party;
* the supervising human or function;
* the operational owner;
* the system or agent operator;
* the incident owner;
* the escalation authority; and
* the party responsible for reviewing significant outcomes.

The specific allocation of responsibility may vary by organizational and legal context.

A.10 Traceability Assessment
----------------------------

The organization should determine what information must be recorded to support oversight and accountability.

Depending on context, records may include:

* delegation authorization;
* agent identity;
* relevant instructions;
* applicable policies or constraints;
* significant decisions;
* material actions;
* external system interactions;
* human interventions;
* escalations;
* exceptions;
* outcomes; and
* changes to delegated authority.

Traceability requirements should be proportionate to the consequences and sensitivity of the delegated activity.

A.11 Readiness Gap Assessment
-----------------------------

A readiness gap exists where the capabilities or controls required for a delegation are not sufficiently established.

Examples include:

* insufficient human understanding;
* inadequate workforce capability;
* unclear accountability;
* inadequate intervention mechanisms;
* excessive delegation boundaries;
* insufficient monitoring;
* inadequate traceability;
* weak governance;
* insufficient incident response; or
* lack of continuous assurance.

A readiness gap should result in an appropriate response.

Possible responses include:

* remediation before delegation;
* reduction of delegated authority;
* additional human oversight;
* additional technical controls;
* additional workforce training;
* enhanced monitoring;
* additional approval requirements; or
* deferral or suspension of delegation.

A.12 Delegation Decision
-----------------------

A HORA-TAI assessment may result in one of the following descriptive outcomes:

**Proceed**

The assessed conditions are sufficient for the proposed delegation under the defined controls.

**Proceed with Conditions**

The delegation may proceed subject to specified controls, remediation actions, monitoring requirements, or additional oversight.

**Reduce Delegation**

The proposed authority should be narrowed until the relevant readiness conditions are satisfied.

**Defer Delegation**

Delegation should not begin until identified readiness gaps have been addressed.

**Suspend Delegation**

Existing delegated authority should be temporarily suspended because readiness conditions or operating conditions are no longer satisfied.

**Terminate Delegation**

Delegated authority should be withdrawn where the organization determines that continued delegation is no longer appropriate.

These outcomes are descriptive decision-support categories and are not intended to establish a universal certification or compliance classification.

A.13 Reassessment Triggers
--------------------------

Reassessment should be considered when one or more of the following occurs:

* material changes to agent capabilities;
* connection of new tools or external systems;
* expansion of delegated authority;
* changes to organizational responsibilities;
* significant workforce changes;
* material incidents or near misses;
* changes in operating environment;
* changes in applicable legal or regulatory requirements;
* significant changes in risk;
* evidence of degraded oversight effectiveness; or
* expiration of a defined assessment period.

Organizations may also establish periodic reassessment intervals appropriate to the delegation context.

A.14 Illustrative HORA-TAI Assessment Record
--------------------------------------------

A structured assessment record may contain the following fields:

**Delegation identifier**

A unique identifier for the delegation being assessed.

**Delegating party**

The human, team, or organization authorizing delegation.

**Agent identifier**

The identity or identifier of the agent or agentic AI system.

**Objective**

The purpose for which authority is being delegated.

**Delegated authority**

The specific actions, decisions, permissions, or capabilities granted to the agent.

**Delegation level**

The proposed HORA-TAI delegation level.

**Operating environment**

The systems, processes, tools, data, and organizational environment in which the agent operates.

**Human oversight**

The responsible human or function and the mechanisms available for supervision and intervention.

**Readiness assessment**

Assessment results for each applicable readiness dimension.

**Readiness gaps**

Identified gaps and associated remediation requirements.

**Controls**

Technical and organizational controls applied to the delegation.

**Accountability**

Identified responsible parties and escalation authorities.

**Assurance evidence**

Evidence supporting the readiness determination.

**Decision**

The resulting delegation decision.

**Reassessment conditions**

Conditions or events that require reassessment.

A.15 Example Assessment
-----------------------

The following example illustrates how the framework may be applied.

An organization proposes to authorize an AI customer-service agent to resolve routine customer requests without obtaining human approval for each individual interaction.

The proposed delegation includes access to customer records, predefined service policies, and a limited ability to issue approved remedies.

The organization identifies the delegation as **Level 2 - Conditional Autonomy**.

The assessment identifies the following conditions:

* the agent has a defined operational scope;
* monetary and operational limits are established;
* requests outside defined conditions are escalated;
* a responsible human function is identified;
* agent actions are logged;
* intervention mechanisms are available;
* relevant staff receive training;
* incidents are reviewed;
* the delegation is subject to periodic reassessment.

The organization also identifies a readiness gap in workforce capacity because the existing supervision function does not have sufficient capacity to review escalated cases within the required timeframe.

The organization therefore establishes additional staffing and escalation procedures before activating the proposed delegation.

This example illustrates that readiness is not determined solely by the capability of the agent. The delegation decision depends on the relationship between agent capability, authority, oversight, organizational capability, and assurance.

A.16 Limitations
----------------

The assessment approach in this annex is an initial proposal.

Further research and validation are required to determine:

* whether the proposed readiness dimensions are sufficiently comprehensive;
* how assessment evidence should be weighted;
* whether quantitative scoring is appropriate;
* how different sectors should adapt the framework;
* how legal and regulatory requirements should be incorporated;
* how readiness can be assessed dynamically;
* how assessment results can be interoperable across organizations; and
* how the framework can integrate with existing AI assurance methodologies.

The framework should therefore be treated as a basis for discussion, experimentation, and refinement rather than as a finalized certification methodology.
